# Mathsense for tutors

_A GCSE maths practice and assessment tool, and what a free trial involves._

---

## In short

Your tutors already set past papers and already tell parents what a student needs
to work on. Mathsense turns that into two things you can use: a printable feedback
sheet per student, and a skill map that builds up over the weeks so you can see
what has actually stuck.

Students practise between sessions on the same account. Everything they do feeds
the same picture.

---

## In person or online

Both work, and the difference is mainly in how you assess. There are two routes
and you can use either or both.

| | **Mark a past paper** | **Set a mini-exam in the app** |
|---|---|---|
| Student does | A real past paper, on paper | A generated paper, on screen |
| Tutor does | Enters the marks they awarded | Opens the finished script and reads it |
| You need | A script to mark from | Nothing — it marks itself |
| Suits | In-person sessions, or homework posted back | Online sessions, or homework between any sessions |

For an **in-person** outfit, marking is the natural fit: students write, tutors
mark, the marks go in. For an **online** outfit, mini-exams usually make more
sense, because nothing physical has to change hands.

**On devices:** students can use a phone. The practice and mini-exam screens are
built for a narrow screen, so an in-person centre with no computers in the room
can still run this on whatever the students have in their pockets.

---

## Marking a past paper

A tutor picks a paper, enters each student's marks question by question, and gets
back:

- **A feedback sheet per student** — "what went well" and "even better if", drawn
  from which questions they dropped marks on, downloadable as a PDF to hand to a
  parent or keep on file.
- **An answer key** for the paper.
- **A mark summary** across the group, so you can see what the whole cohort found
  hard.

If the students are in a Mathsense class, those marks also become part of each
student's skill map. Full marks on a question counts as secure; a dropped mark
never pulls a skill down, so one bad morning does not undo a term of evidence.

**Papers available now:** 42.

| Board | Papers |
|---|---|
| AQA 8300 | All three papers, both tiers — June 2023, Nov 2023, June 2024, Nov 2024, June 2025 (30 papers) |
| Edexcel 1MA1 | All three papers, both tiers — June 2025 (6 papers) |
| OCR J560 | All six papers — June 2025 (6 papers) |

You do not need an account to use the marking tool. It is free and it stays free;
if you want to try one thing before reading any further, try that.

## Mini-exams

A student sits a generated paper on screen under exam conditions. It is marked
automatically, and a tutor of their class can open the finished script and see
every question and every answer the student gave.

**The marking is honest about its own limits, which matters if you are going to
rely on it.** The score comes back as a confirmed floor plus a band: every mark
it can actually prove from the answer is counted, and method marks it cannot
prove are flagged as unknown rather than quietly awarded or quietly withheld. A
tutor reading the script resolves that band by eye — which, in an online session,
is a reasonable thing to do with the student watching.

The one constraint worth knowing in advance: **a tutor cannot overwrite a mark
the app has recorded.** They can read the script and disagree with it in the
session, but the recorded score stands as the app computed it.

## What builds up over time

Each student has a skill map across the GCSE maths curriculum, built from three
sources:

1. **Their own practice**, done at home or between sessions.
2. **Work a tutor sets them** as an assignment.
3. **Papers a tutor has marked** for them, and mini-exams they have sat.

A tutor with a class sees the group's mastery by skill, and can scope it to the
topics they have actually taught, so the percentages mean something rather than
counting everything a student has never been shown.

## How students join

A tutor creates a class and gets a short join code. Students enter it once, and
that is the whole process. The code can be changed if it gets out.

Online, the tutor pastes the code into the chat or shares their screen while the
student signs up. In person, it is read out, and the signing-up usually wants to
happen on a device the student has with them, or as the first piece of homework.

**One thing to know in advance, because it shapes everything else:** students
create their own Mathsense accounts. You cannot create accounts for them, and
neither can we on your behalf.

That is deliberate rather than a gap. A student's account is theirs: it exists
before you, it carries on if they stop coming to you, and the decision to show
their record to a tutor is the student's own, made by entering the code and
reversible by leaving the class. It is why we are able to say plainly that you
are not handing us a list of children, and that nothing about a student's account
is ours to hand to anybody else.

The practical cost is that getting a group started takes a few minutes of everyone
signing up, and for under-16s that usually means a parent's email address. There
is no spreadsheet import and no link to any school system. For a handful of
students this is a non-issue; it would not scale to a secondary school, and we are
not pretending otherwise.

## What a tutor can and cannot see

**Can see:** the student's chosen display name, their year group if they gave one,
when they joined, their skill map, work set for them, papers marked for them, and
any mini-exam they sat — in full, questions and answers.

**Cannot see:** their email address, their password, or a transcript of their
ordinary practice. For practice a tutor gets the skill map, never a record of
which questions were attempted or what was typed. Mini-exams are the exception and
are shown in full, because marking one means reading it.

Two further points worth stating rather than leaving to be discovered:

- **Joining shares the whole record, not just what happens next.** A student's
  skill map is built from everything they have ever done on Mathsense, including
  practice from before they joined your class.
- **The record belongs to the student.** If a student deletes their account, the
  papers you marked for them go too. Your marking is evidence about them, held in
  their account, not an assessment record we hold for you.

## What is covered, honestly

The practice bank is 271 published questions. It is deepest where the exam marks
are concentrated and thin in places — there are topics where it has only one or
two questions, and it is not yet a complete substitute for a textbook.

Rather than claim otherwise: **send me the topics your tutors are covering this
term and I will tell you exactly which are well covered and which are not**,
before you commit anyone's time.

## The free trial

- **Free for a half-term, around eight weeks**, for as many students as you want
  to put on it.
- Every student in your tutors' classes gets the **full paid feature set** for the
  whole window — unlimited mini-exams, practice targeted at a chosen skill or
  topic, and their weak spots shown rather than hidden.
- **No card, no invoice, no obligation**, and nothing switches to a paid plan on
  its own.
- **When the window ends**, the extra features stop. Nothing is deleted: accounts,
  skill maps, marked papers and sat mini-exams all stay exactly as they are, and a
  student can carry on with the free version.

**What I would like out of it.** Two things, and I would rather say so than
pretend a trial is a favour. First, whether students come back between sessions —
the honest position is that most individual sign-ups do not, and a tutor who sets
the expectation is the thing I most need to test. Second, whichever of the two
assessment routes fits how you work: if you mark papers, nobody has yet recorded
one through the product, so you would be the first, and anything awkward about it
is worth more to me than a polite review.

**Pricing afterwards is not fixed**, and I would rather agree it with you once you
know whether the thing is useful than quote a number now and defend it.

## What it does not do yet

- No import of students from a spreadsheet or a school system.
- No admin view across your whole organisation — a tutor's classes sit on that
  tutor's own login, and moving a class between tutors, or switching off a tutor
  who leaves, is something I do by hand on request.
- No marking of anything outside the papers listed above.
- No written-method marking. The paper tool records the marks a tutor awards; it
  does not read a student's working.
- A tutor cannot override a mini-exam mark the app has recorded.
- It is not a whiteboard and does not replace whatever you run sessions on. It
  sits alongside Zoom, Teams or a table.
- Edexcel and OCR coverage is June 2025 only. AQA goes back to 2023.

## Data protection, briefly

We are the controller of a student's account, because the student creates it and
we run it. What a tutor sees, they see because the student chose to show them, and
what you then do with it is yours. We are not acting as your processor, because
there is no mechanism by which you could instruct us about a student's account
even if you wanted to.

- Student records hold **no email address at all** — there is no such field. It is
  held by the authentication system and is not reachable by any tutor-facing
  screen.
- **No special category data** is collected: nothing on health, SEND, ethnicity or
  anything similar. There are no fields for it.
- Database and hosting are in **London**. Sign-up and password emails go via a
  French provider with EU servers. Payment goes via Stripe. Optional reminder
  emails use a US provider, and only for students who opted in. Google sign-in is
  available and involves Google if a student chooses it.
- **Inactive student accounts are deleted after one year**, along with everything
  in them.
- The full privacy notice is at `mathsense.net/privacy`, and there is a longer
  written position on roles, sub-processors and security that I can send you if
  your side wants to read it properly.

If you hold data about these students yourself — and as a tutoring business you
almost certainly do — that stays your responsibility and is not changed by
anything here.

## Who you are dealing with

Mathsense is a sole trader, not a company with an account manager. That has an
upside and a downside, and you should know both: the downside is that there is no
service level agreement, no 24-hour support line and no professional indemnity
cover to point at. The upside is that if something is wrong or missing, you are
talking to the person who can change it, and during a trial that is worth more
than a support queue.
