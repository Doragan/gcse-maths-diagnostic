## The spreadsheet you built

You described putting past paper marks into Excel and having it tell you which
topics to work on. That's the thing Mathsense does best, and it's the part I'd
want you to try first.

A tutor picks the paper, enters each student's marks question by question, and
gets back:

- **A feedback sheet per student** — what went well, even better if — as a PDF
  you can send to a parent or keep.
- **An answer key** for the paper.
- **A mark summary** across everyone who sat it.
- Each student's **skill map updated automatically** — full marks on a question
  counts as secure, and a dropped mark never pulls a skill down, so one bad
  morning doesn't undo a term's evidence.

66 papers are in there: AQA across five series both tiers, Edexcel June 2024,
November 2024 and June 2025 both tiers, and OCR June 2023, 2024 and 2025 — all
three boards, both tiers, going back three years.

**You don't need an account to use this and you never will.** If you try one
thing, try this — it's the closest thing to your spreadsheet, and it works today.

## Getting them signed up

You said they'd never link their own accounts, and that you'd rather make the
accounts and hand out the logins. I understand why — that's how tutorbird works
for you, and it's the normal way round in your world.

I'm not going to be able to do exactly that, and I'd rather tell you why than
quietly not build it. If I create an account and you hold the password, then I
can't honestly tell a parent that their child's record is only visible to you
because the child agreed to it — and that one sentence is what keeps me out of a
much heavier legal arrangement with every tutor and school I ever work with.

**What I'm building instead, and it should be ready for your trial:** you type in
the students you're expecting, the system emails each of them a link, they click
it and pick a password. You see a list of who's joined and **who hasn't**, so you
can chase the stragglers — which is the bit you actually wanted and the bit the
current join-code approach can't do.

For Year 9s I'd suggest inviting a parent's email address rather than the
student's. It's less chasing, and it puts the consent where it belongs anyway.

If that still turns out to be too much friction once you've tried it, tell me —
that's exactly the kind of thing the trial is for.

## What I can't help you with

You said primary and Year 9 are most of your work. Mathsense covers **GCSE
Foundation and Higher, and nothing below it.** There's no primary content and no
Key Stage 3 content, and accounts are 13+, so primary is out on both counts. That
isn't a gap I can close quickly — it's a different product.

So be clear-eyed: I'm useful for a minority of what you do. Your Year 9s and your
GCSE students, not the rest.

Also not happening:

- **English.** Maths only.
- **Annotating on your screen while a student works**, MyMaths-style. There's
  nothing like it and it isn't close. For primary especially, MyMaths is doing
  something I'm not.
- **iGCSE, today.** I checked after we spoke: the course definitions exist in the
  system but nothing is switched on for it — no iGCSE practice and no 4MA1 papers
  in the marking tool. I'm part-way through the groundwork, and knowing you have
  iGCSE students moves it up my list, but I'm not going to tell you it's there
  when it isn't.

## What you'd see, and whose data it is

**You'd see:** their display name, year group, when they joined, their skill map,
work you set, papers you marked, and any mini-exam they sat — in full, every
question and every answer.

**You wouldn't see:** their email address, their password, or a transcript of
their ordinary practice. For practice you get the skill map, never a record of
what they typed. Mini-exams are the exception, because marking one means reading
it.

Two things worth knowing rather than discovering:

- **Joining shares the whole record**, including practice from before they joined
  your group.
- **If a student deletes their account, the papers you marked for them go too.**
  It's their record, not my archive of your assessments.

Inactive accounts are deleted after a year. The full privacy notice is at
mathsense.net/privacy, and there's a longer written position on roles and
sub-processors if you ever want it.
