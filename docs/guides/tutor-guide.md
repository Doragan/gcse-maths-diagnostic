# Mathsense — Quick Start

**For tutors. About 15 minutes to get a student working.**

---

### Want to try it before signing up for anything?

Go to **mathsense.net/mark**, take a paper you've already marked, and type the
marks in. It'll tell you which skills those dropped marks point at, and give you
a feedback sheet per student.

No account, no sign-up, nothing to cancel. Jump to **Part 4**.

---

## What's in this guide

| Part | | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Set yourself up | 3 min |
| **2** | Get your students in | 5 min |
| **3** | Get them answering questions | 5 min |
| **4** | Mark a paper | 10 min |
| **5** | See how they're doing | 2 min |

You don't need to do these in order. Parts 1–3 go together; Part 4 works on its
own.

# Part 1 · Set yourself up

## Step 1 — Create your account

Go to **mathsense.net/auth**

Sign up with an email address and password, or use **Sign in with Google**.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the sign-up page*

---

## Step 2 — Create a class

From your dashboard: **Classes** → **New class**

Give it a name — "Year 10 Tuesday", "GCSE Higher group", anything you like —
then press **Create**.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the New class box with a name typed in*

### ⚠️ If you teach one-to-one, read this bit

**Make one class per student, named after the student.**

It looks strange, and you'd be forgiven for putting all eight of your
individual students into one class called "My students". Don't — it'll report
the wrong thing, and it won't warn you.

Here's why. Later on you can tick off the topics you've taught, and progress
gets measured against that list. **That list belongs to the class, not the
student.** Eight students in one class share one list, so the percentages end
up measured against topics most of them have never been taught.

One class each, and everything lines up.

# Part 2 · Get your students in

Two ways in. **Email invitations are better if you want to know who hasn't
joined yet** — otherwise either is fine.

## Option A — Invite by email *(recommended)*

**Your class** → **People** → **Invite by email**

**1.** Paste in the email addresses — commas, spaces, or one per line.

**2.** Press **Invite**.

**3.** Each student gets an email with a link. They click it, choose their own
password, and accept.

**4.** You'll see **Invited (3 of 8 joined)** — so you always know who still
needs a nudge.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the invite box, and the "3 of 8 joined" list underneath*

## Option B — Give out the class code

**Your class** → **People** → **Join code**

It's four characters, like `MX4T`.

**1.** Send your students to **mathsense.net/student?mode=signup** to create an
account.

**2.** Once they're in, they go to **My classes** and type the code.

> 💡 **Keep the `?mode=signup` on the end of that link.** Without it they land
> on a login page, and the sign-up link is tucked underneath where most people
> don't spot it.

> ⚠️ **Not mathsense.net/join** — that's an older page for something else. The
> class code goes in **My classes**, on their own dashboard.

---

## Three things worth knowing before you invite a whole group

**Inviting doesn't create accounts.** Students sign up themselves and accept the
invitation. That's deliberate — it's what makes the account theirs, and it's why
I can't just hand you a list of ready-made logins.

**They can use any email address**, not only the one you invited.

**For anyone under 16**, invite a parent's address and let them pass the link on.

# Part 3 · Get them answering questions

Three ways. You don't have to choose — most tutors end up using all three.

| | Who needs an account | Is it saved? | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Run a session** | Just you | ✗ No | A first lesson, or anyone reluctant to sign up |
| **B · Let them practise** | Both of you | ✓ Yes | The everyday default |
| **C · Set them work** | Both of you | ✓ Yes | Everyone on the same thing |

---

## A · Run a session — nobody has to sign up

The fastest way to see it working, and the easiest thing to put in front of a
student who doesn't want an account.

**You:** Dashboard → **Run a lesson** → **Start a session**

Choose the qualification and how many questions each, then **Start session**.
You'll get a short code.

**Them:** they go to **mathsense.net/s**, type the code and a first name.

That's the whole thing. No email, no password, nothing to install. You watch
their answers come in — right and wrong — as they work.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the live session view with the code showing*

> ⚠️ **Two time limits.** The session **stops accepting answers 6 hours after
> you start it**, so a 9am session is dead by 3pm — just start a new one. And
> everything is **deleted the same evening**. It's excellent for a lesson and no
> use at all as a record.

---

## B · Let them practise — the one that actually matters

Once a student is in your class, they can just use it. They pick a skill or a
topic, answer questions, and get the answer explained whenever they're wrong.

Nothing for you to set up. **This is what most students do most of the time, and
it's what builds the skill map you'll be looking at in Part 5.**

> 💡 If you do one thing after they join, it's this: tell them to go and
> practise something. Ten minutes is enough to start showing you anything.

---

## C · Set them specific work

Dashboard → **Assignments** → **Create**

Four sections to fill in:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Details** | Name it, and say when it's due |
| **Questions** | Choose what's on it |
| **Completion rules** | What counts as finished |
| **Assign to** | Who gets it |

Then **Create assignment**.

> 💡 Worth it when you want everyone doing the same thing. Not worth bothering
> with in the first week — let them practise first.

# Part 4 · Mark a paper

**This is the part closest to the spreadsheet you already use, and it needs no
account at all.**

Go to **mathsense.net/mark**

## What it does — and what it doesn't

**It doesn't mark anything.** It holds no question text, no answers and no mark
scheme. **You mark the paper yourself, exactly as you do now.**

What it has is every question of 66 past papers tagged with the skills it tests
— all 2,466 of them, read and coded by hand. So when you type in the marks, it
can tell you the thing the marks alone can't: **which skills those dropped marks
point at.**

## Step by step

**1. Choose the paper.** 66 of them — AQA, Edexcel and OCR, both tiers, back to
2023.

**2. Leave "They sat the whole paper" ticked**, unless they only did part of it.

**3. Paste your student names**, one per line, then **Add these students**.

> 💡 Prefer Excel? **Download CSV template** gives you a spreadsheet with the
> question numbers and marks already in it. Fill it in, then **Import marks from
> CSV**.

**4. Type the marks in.** Hover over any question number to see which skill it
tests.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the marks grid with a few rows filled in*

**5. Watch "The class so far" build as you type.** Nothing to press. You get the
average, median, highest and lowest, a breakdown by topic, and —the useful one—
**Questions to revisit**, ordered so the biggest lesson is at the top.

This bit is yours. It never appears on a student's sheet.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the "Questions to revisit" list*

**6. Press "Get feedback sheets."** One PDF, a page per student: **what went
well** and **what to work on**.

---

## Three things to know

**⚠️ Blank and zero are the same thing.** Leaving a box empty doesn't take the
question out — it still counts as nought out of however many marks it was worth.
To genuinely leave questions out, untick **They sat the whole paper** and
deselect them.

**⚠️ Nothing is saved.** It all lives in that browser tab and vanishes when you
close it. Download the PDF first.

**💡 For your first go, pick an AQA June 2025 paper.** Those sheets come with
practice questions to hand out, and most papers don't have them yet. (They're
coming — it's hand-written work, and that's where I've got to.)

---

## Making it count towards their skill map

The version above is a one-off. To have a paper update a student's record:

**Your class** → **Papers** → **Record a marked paper**

Same grid. The difference is that it saves, and each student's skill map
updates — **full marks counts as secure, and a dropped mark never pulls a skill
down.** A bad morning can't undo a term's evidence.

# Part 5 · See how they're doing

Dashboard → **Your students**

Two numbers per student:

| | |
|---|---|
| **mastered** | Skills they've shown they can do |
| **to work on** | Skills they've had a proper go at and aren't getting right yet |

Sort by **Quietest first** to find whoever has drifted — usually more useful
than finding whoever is struggling loudly, because they'll tell you themselves.

Tap any student for the full breakdown.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the Your students list*

> ⚠️ **"To work on" needs five attempts** at a skill before it'll say anything.
> Below that it sits quietly as "in progress". So the number barely moves at
> first — that's normal, not a sign nothing's happening.

### Once you're past the first week

Open a class and there's more: mastery by topic, progress over time, and
**Topics covered** — tick off what you've actually taught, and progress gets
measured against that instead of the whole GCSE. Worth doing, or the percentages
look bleak for no good reason.

# Quick reference

| Where | What it's for | Sign-in? |
|---|---|---|
| **mathsense.net/mark** | Mark a paper, get feedback sheets | No |
| **mathsense.net/auth** | Your account | — |
| **mathsense.net/student?mode=signup** | Where students sign up | — |
| **mathsense.net/s** | Where students join a session | No |
| **My classes** *(their dashboard)* | Where students enter a class code | Yes |

## The five things that catch people out

1. **One class per student** if you teach one-to-one.
2. **The class code goes in My classes** — not mathsense.net/join.
3. **Sessions stop after 6 hours.** Just start another.
4. **Inviting isn't joining.** Nothing appears until they've signed up and
   accepted.
5. **"To work on" needs five goes** at a skill before it shows up.
