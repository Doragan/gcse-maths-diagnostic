# Mathsense for tutors — how to actually use it

_Written for Dani, October 2026. Every screen name, button and rule in here was
checked against the live site and the code rather than written from memory;
where something doesn't work the way you'd expect, it says so._

---

## Start here: who needs an account

| What you want to do | You | Them | Kept afterwards? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mark a paper and get feedback sheets | **No** | **No** | No — gone when you close the tab |
| Run a session where students answer questions | **Yes** | **No** | No — deleted the same day |
| Mark a paper **and have it update their skill map** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See a student's progress over weeks | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Set work for a student | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Only one thing needs nobody to sign up: marking a paper. **Running a session
needs an account for you but nothing from the students** — that's the useful
middle ground. Everything that is *remembered* needs accounts on both sides.

If you just want to see what the feedback sheets look like before committing to
anything, skip to **§3.1** — no sign-up, five minutes. Otherwise start at the
top.

---

## 1. Setting yourself up

### Your account

**mathsense.net/auth** — email and password, or Google. Two minutes.

### A class

**Your dashboard → Classes → New class.** Name it whatever you like.

> ⚠️ **For one-to-one, make one class per student.** It looks odd, and I'd
> rather tell you than let you find out, because it fails quietly.
>
> **Topics covered** (§5) is set per class, not per student. Put eight
> individuals in one "class" and they share a single taught-list, so the
> mastery percentages are measured against the wrong thing for all of them —
> with no warning. Their individual skill maps are still their own; it's the
> denominator that goes wrong. Name each class after the student.

## 2. Getting your students in

Two ways. The first is better if you want to know who hasn't joined.

### Invite by email

**Your class → People → Invite by email.** Paste the addresses — commas, spaces
or one per line — and press **Invite**.

Each student gets an email with a link. They click it, choose their own
password, and accept. You then see **Invited (3 of 8 joined)**, so you know who
to chase.

### Or hand out the join code

Same page, under **Join code** — four characters, like `MX4T`. The student
signs up, goes to **My classes**, and types it in. Nothing for you to collect,
but no list of who hasn't joined.

> Not to be confused with mathsense.net/join, which is an older route for a
> different thing. The class code goes in **My classes** on their own dashboard.

### Three things to know before you invite thirty people

- **Inviting creates no accounts.** They sign up themselves and accept. That's
  deliberate — it's what makes the account theirs and the sharing consented, and
  it's why I can't hand you a list of ready-made logins.
- **They can sign up with any address**, not only the one you invited.
- **For anyone under 16, invite a parent's address** and let them pass the link
  on. Signing up asks the student to confirm they're 13 or over.

## 3. Marking a paper

Two versions, and they are the same grid. The difference is whether anything is
kept.

| | §3.1 Quick | §3.2 Recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Where | **mathsense.net/mark** | Your class → **Papers** |
| Accounts | None, ever | You and the student |
| Feedback sheets | Yes | Yes |
| Updates their skill map | **No** | **Yes** |
| Still there tomorrow | No | Yes |

> **What this tool does, and doesn't.** It does not mark anything. The system
> holds no question text, no images, no answers and no mark scheme — only each
> question's number, what it's out of, and which skill it tests. **You mark the
> paper yourself, as you do now, and type the marks in.** What you get back is
> the thing the marks can't tell you on their own: which skills they point at.
>
> That's also why it needs no account and saves nothing — the papers are built
> into the page, the marking stays in the browser, and the PDF is made there
> too. Nothing is ever sent anywhere, so there's nothing to log in to and
> nothing to come back to.

### 3.1 The quick version — mathsense.net/mark

**1. Pick the paper.** 66 of them: AQA, Edexcel and OCR, both tiers, back to
2023.

**2. Leave "They sat the whole paper" ticked** — unless they only did part of
it. This tick matters more than it looks; see *Blank, zero and leaving a
question out* below.

**3. Paste your names**, one per line — a column out of a register works — then
**Add these students**.

> There's also **Download CSV template** / **Import marks from CSV** if you'd
> rather start in Excel. The template already has the question numbers and the
> marks available.

**4. Type the marks in.** Hover any question heading to see which skill it
tests.

**5. Watch "The class so far" fill in as you type.** Nothing to press. Average,
median, lowest, highest, a breakdown by topic, and — the useful one —
**Questions to revisit**, ordered by the marks the group lost, biggest lesson
first. This part is for you and never appears on a student's sheet.

**6. Press "Get feedback sheets."** One PDF: a page per student with what went
well, what to work on, and up to three questions to practise — plus an answer
key as a final page for you.

> ⚠️ **That answer key is for the practice questions on the sheets, not for the
> exam paper.** It's so you can mark the follow-up work without doing it
> yourself. For the paper, you need the board's own mark scheme, same as now.

> Anyone who scored **80% or more** also gets one harder question to push on.
> Below that they don't, deliberately: a student who dropped a third of the
> paper doesn't need stretching, and the sheet stays a page.

> ⚠️ **Nothing here is saved.** It lives in that browser tab and is gone when
> you close it. Download the PDF first.

### 3.2 The recorded version — your class → Papers

**Record a marked paper.** Same grid, but when you save it:

- Each student's **skill map updates automatically**.
- **Full marks on a question counts as secure** for the skills it tests.
- **A dropped mark never pulls a skill down.**

That last rule is deliberate and stronger than it sounds — see the next section.
A paper is a bad day as much as it is a measurement, so it can only ever add
evidence, never remove it.

### Blank, zero, and leaving a question out

This is the one thing on the marking screen I'd ignore. The page tells you a
blank and a zero are different, and that "only a zero counts against the
student". **They are not different, and neither counts against them.**

- **For the feedback sheet and the class summary**, a blank is scored as a zero.
  Both give 0 out of whatever the question was worth.
- **For the skill map**, neither does anything at all. A wrong or partial answer
  on a paper is ignored entirely — only full marks register.

So leaving a cell blank does **not** take the question out of their score.

**If they genuinely didn't sit part of the paper**, untick **They sat the whole
paper** and deselect those questions. That is the only thing that removes them
from the total, the percentages and the feedback.

## 4. Running a session — no sign-up for the students

Use this when asking students for email addresses is more trouble than it's
worth. You need your account from §1; they need nothing at all.

**You:** **Your dashboard → Run a lesson → Start a session.** Choose the
qualification and how many questions each, then **Start session**. You get a
short code.

**Them:** **mathsense.net/s**, type the code and a first name. No email, no
password, no age question, nothing to install.

**While it runs:** you see who's answering and what they're getting right and
wrong, live. There's a display view with the code in large type for a screen.

> ⚠️ **Two clocks, and the first one will catch you out.**
>
> **The session stops working 6 hours after you start it.** Start one at 9am and
> it won't accept answers after 3pm — the code stops working mid-afternoon. For
> a second session that day, start a new one.
>
> **The data is deleted the same evening**, by a job that runs at 22:40 UTC. No
> skill map, no history, nothing to come back to. This is a way to run a
> session, not a way to keep a record.

## 5. What you'll see once they're working

**Your dashboard → Your students.** Two numbers per student:

- **mastered** — skills they've shown they can do
- **to work on** — skills they've had a real go at and aren't getting right yet

> "To work on" needs **five attempts** at a skill before it will say anything.
> Under five, a skill sits quietly as *in progress*. So a student who tried
> something twice and got it wrong both times won't show up here yet — which is
> why the number barely moves in the first session or two.

Sort by **Most to work on** or **Quietest first**. The second is usually more
useful: it finds the student who's drifted, not the one struggling loudly. Tap a
student for the full breakdown.

> ⚠️ **"To work on" going up is not bad news.** It means they've attempted
> something new and not got it yet. Early on, expect both numbers to rise. A
> student who has done nothing has a clean-looking profile and no evidence at
> all, which is worse.
>
> That's why these are counts rather than percentages. A percentage of the whole
> GCSE starts near zero and crawls, which makes a student working hard look
> stuck.

### Inside a class

A class page also shows average mastery by topic, mastery over time, and
**Topics covered** — mark what you've actually taught and mastery is measured
against that instead of the whole course. Worth doing early, or the percentages
look bleak for no good reason.

## 6. What students get

- **Practice** on any skill or topic, with instant feedback and worked
  explanations.
- **Mini-exams** — built from our own question bank, but **shaped like a real
  paper**: 80 marks, the same spread of marks per question, and a different
  balance for Foundation and Higher, all taken from 30 real papers we coded
  question by question. The questions are ours; the shape of the paper is
  theirs. If you're expecting scanned past paper questions, that isn't what
  this is.
- **Their own skill map.**
- Anything you set them.

They don't see each other's work.

## 7. What you can and can't see

**You see:** their display name, year group, when they joined, their skill map,
work you set, papers you marked for them, and any mini-exam they sat — every
question and answer.

**You don't see:** their email address, their password, or a transcript of their
ordinary practice. For practice you get the skill map, never a record of what
they typed. Mini-exams are the exception, because marking one means reading it.

Two things worth knowing rather than discovering:

- **Joining shares their whole record**, including practice from before they
  joined you.
- **If a student deletes their account, the papers you marked for them go too.**
  It's their record, not my archive of your assessments.

**They can leave your class whenever they want**, from **My classes**, and
nobody can be added to a class without accepting. Leaving stops the sharing
from that point on — it doesn't delete what you've already marked.

Inactive accounts are deleted after a year of not signing in. Full notice at
**mathsense.net/privacy**.

## 8. Things that will catch you out

| | |
|---|---|
| **One class per student for 1:1** | One class holding all of them shares a single "Topics covered", so every percentage is measured against the wrong list |
| **Inviting creates no accounts** | They must click the link and sign up before you see anything |
| **Blank is the same as zero** | To take a question out, untick "They sat the whole paper" and deselect it |
| **"To work on" needs 5 attempts** | Fewer than that and the skill stays quietly "in progress" |
| **/mark saves nothing** | Download the PDF before closing the tab |
| **Sessions stop after 6 hours** | And are deleted the same evening. They need your account, not the students' |
| **"To work on" rising is normal** | It means they're attempting things |
| **Mark "Topics covered"** | Otherwise mastery is measured against the whole course |

---

## 9. What isn't there

- **Nothing below GCSE.** No primary, no Key Stage 3, and accounts are 13+.
- **No English.** Maths only.
- **No annotating a student's work while they do it**, MyMaths-style.
- **iGCSE is partial.** Foundation practice is well covered; Higher is covered
  apart from **calculus — differentiation, stationary points and kinematics have
  no questions at all yet.** And **none of the 66 markable papers are 4MA1.**
