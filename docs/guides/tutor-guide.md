# Mathsense for tutors — how to actually use it

_Written for Dani, October 2026. Every screen name and button in here was
checked against the live site rather than written from memory; where something
doesn't work the way you'd expect, it says so._

---

## Start here: what needs an account and what doesn't

This is the thing most likely to confuse you, so it's first.

| What you want to do | You need an account | They do | Kept afterwards? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mark a paper and get feedback sheets | **No** | **No** | No — gone when you close the tab |
| Run a session where students answer questions | **Yes** | **No** | No — deleted the same day |
| Mark a paper **and have it update a student's skill map** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See a student's progress over weeks | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Set work for a student | Yes | Yes | Yes |

**The short version:** only one thing here needs nobody to sign up at all, and
that's marking a paper. **Running a session needs an account for you but nothing
from the students** — which is the useful middle ground. Everything that is
remembered afterwards needs accounts on both sides.

My suggestion is to work down that table — try the marking with no sign-up at
all, make yourself an account when you want to run a session, and only ask
students to sign up once you've decided it's worth it.

## 1. Mark a paper (no account, five minutes)

This is the one closest to your spreadsheet. **mathsense.net/mark**

**1. Pick the paper.** There are 66: AQA, Edexcel and OCR, both tiers, going back
to 2023. Pick the one they actually sat.

**2. Leave "They sat the whole paper" ticked**, unless they only did part of it —
untick it and you can choose which questions to include.

**3. Paste your names.** One per line. A column copied out of a register works.
Then **Add these students**.

> There's also **Download CSV template** / **Import marks from CSV** if you'd
> rather work in Excel first. The template already has the question numbers and
> the marks available, so it may suit you better than typing.

**4. Type the marks in**, question by question.

> ⚠️ **Blank and zero are not the same thing.** Leave a question blank if they
> didn't attempt it — a blank is ignored, a zero counts against them. Getting
> this wrong is the easiest way to make a student look worse than they are.
>
> Hover over any question heading to see which skill it tests.

**5. Watch "The class so far" fill in as you type.** You don't have to finish or
press anything. You get the average, median, lowest and highest, a breakdown by
topic, and — the useful one — **Questions to revisit**, ordered by how many marks
the group lost. The biggest lesson is at the top.

This section is for you. It is not on the students' sheets.

**6. Press "Get feedback sheets."** You get one PDF: a page per student with what
went well, what to work on, questions to practise and one harder question to push
on — plus the answer key as a final page for you, never on a student's sheet.

### Two things to know

- **Nothing is saved.** It lives in that browser tab and disappears when you close
  it. Download the PDF before you go.
- **This does not update anybody's skill map**, because there's nobody to update.
  That needs accounts — see section 5.

## 2. Run a session — no sign-up for the students

Use this for a first session, or any time asking students for email addresses is
more trouble than it's worth.

> **You need your own account for this** — it lives in your dashboard, so sign up
> first (section 3, two minutes). **The students need nothing at all:** no email,
> no password, no sign-up.

**You:** go to **Your dashboard → Run a lesson → Start a session**. Choose the
qualification and how many questions each, press **Start session**. You get a
short code.

**Them:** go to **mathsense.net/s**, type the code and a first name. That's it.
No email, no password, no age question, nothing to install.

**While it runs:** you see who's answering and what they're getting right and
wrong, live. There's a **display** view with the code in large type if you want
it on a screen.

> ⚠️ **It deletes itself at the end of the day.** No skill map, no history,
> nothing to come back to. This is a way to run a session, not a way to keep a
> record. If a student wants their work kept, they need an account.

## 3. Signing up, and getting students signed up

### You

**mathsense.net/auth** — email and password, or Google. That's the tutor account.

### Them

Two ways, and the first is better:

**Invite by email.** Open your class → **People** → **Invite by email**. Paste the
addresses — commas, spaces or one per line. Press **Invite**. Each student gets an
email with a link; they click it, choose their own password, and accept.

You then see **Invited (3 of 8 joined)** — so you know exactly who to chase. That
list is the bit you said you wanted.

**Or give out the join code.** Same page, under **Join code**. They go to
**mathsense.net/join** and type it. Fewer emails to collect; no list of who hasn't
joined yet.

### Three things worth knowing before you invite thirty people

- **No accounts are created by inviting.** They sign up themselves and accept.
  That's deliberate — it's what makes the account theirs and the sharing
  consented, and it's why I can't just hand you a list of logins.
- **They can sign up with any address**, not only the one you invited. So a
  student who'd rather use their own email can.
- **For anyone under 16, invite a parent's address** and let them pass the link
  on. Signing up asks the student to confirm they're 13 or over.

## 4. Setting up a class — including for one-to-one

**Your dashboard → Classes → New class.** Name it whatever you like ("Year 10 Set
2", or just the student's name).

**For 1:1 tuition, make one class per student.** It looks odd, but it's how the
system keeps their work separate, and the student list works properly that way.
Don't put all your individual students in one "class".

## 5. Marking a paper so it actually counts

Section 1 gives you feedback sheets and forgets everything. This version
remembers.

**Your class → Papers → Record a marked paper.** Same marking grid, but when you
save it:

- Each student's **skill map updates automatically**.
- **Full marks on a question counts as secure** for the skills it tests.
- **A dropped mark never pulls a skill down.** One bad morning doesn't undo a
  term's evidence.

That last rule is deliberate. A paper is a bad day as much as a measurement, so
it can only ever add evidence, never remove it.

## 6. What you'll see once they're working

**Your dashboard → Your students.** For each student, two numbers:

- **mastered** — skills they've shown they can do
- **to work on** — skills they've attempted and are getting wrong

You can sort by **Most to work on** or **Quietest first**. The second one is
usually the more useful: it finds the student who's drifted, not the one who's
struggling loudly.

Tap a student for their full breakdown.

> ⚠️ **"To work on" going up is not bad news.** It means they've attempted
> something new and not got it yet. Early on, expect both numbers to rise. A
> student who's done nothing has a clean-looking profile and no evidence at all —
> which is worse, not better.
>
> This is why the figures are counts rather than percentages. A percentage of the
> whole GCSE starts near zero and crawls, which makes a student who is working
> hard look stuck.

### Inside a class

A class page also shows average mastery by topic, a mastery-over-time line, and
**Topics covered** — mark what you've actually taught, and mastery is measured
against that instead of the whole course. Worth doing early, or the percentages
will look bleak for no good reason.

---

## 7. What students get

- **Practice** on any skill or topic, with instant feedback and worked
  explanations.
- **Mini-exams** — assembled from real past paper questions.
- **Their own skill map**, so they can see what to work on without being told.
- Anything you set them.

They **don't** see each other's work.

---

## 8. What you can and can't see

**You see:** their display name, year group, when they joined, their skill map,
work you set, papers you marked for them, and any mini-exam they sat — every
question and answer.

**You don't see:** their email address, their password, or a transcript of their
ordinary practice. For practice you get the skill map, never a record of what they
typed. Mini-exams are the exception, because marking one means reading it.

Two things worth knowing rather than discovering:

- **Joining shares their whole record**, including practice from before they
  joined you.
- **If a student deletes their account, the papers you marked for them go too.**
  It's their record, not my archive of your assessments.

Inactive accounts are deleted after a year. Full notice at
**mathsense.net/privacy**.

## 9. Things that will catch you out

| | |
|---|---|
| **Blank ≠ zero** when entering marks | A blank is "didn't attempt" and is ignored; a zero counts against them |
| **/mark saves nothing** | Download the PDF before closing the tab |
| **Sessions vanish the same day** | Fine for a lesson, useless as a record. They need your account, not the students' |
| **One class per student for 1:1** | Not one class with all your individuals in it |
| **"To work on" rising is normal** | It means they're attempting things |
| **Mark "Topics covered"** | Otherwise mastery is measured against the whole course and looks worse than it is |
| **Inviting doesn't create accounts** | They have to click the link and sign up before you see anything |

---

## 10. What isn't there

Said plainly so you don't go looking.

- **Nothing below GCSE.** No primary, no Key Stage 3, and accounts are 13+. Most
  of your work is outside what this covers.
- **No English.** Maths only.
- **No annotating a student's work while they do it**, MyMaths-style.
- **iGCSE is partial.** Foundation practice is well covered; Higher is covered
  apart from **calculus — differentiation, stationary points and kinematics have
  no questions at all yet.** And **none of the 66 markable papers are 4MA1**, so
  you can't mark an iGCSE paper the way you can a GCSE one.
