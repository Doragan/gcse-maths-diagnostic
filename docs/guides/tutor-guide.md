# Mathsense — quick start

_Enough to get going. There's more to it, but none of it matters yet._

---

## 1. Sign yourself up — 2 minutes

**mathsense.net/auth** — email and password, or sign in with Google.

---

## 2. Make a class — 1 minute

**Dashboard → Classes → New class.** Call it anything.

> **Doing one-to-one? Make one class per student**, named after them. One class
> holding all your individuals will quietly report the wrong thing.

---

## 3. Get your students in

Two ways. Either is fine.

**Invite by email** — *your class → People → Invite by email.* Paste the
addresses, press **Invite**. They get a link, sign up, and accept. You see
**Invited (3 of 8 joined)**, so you know who to chase.

**Or give them the code** — same page, under **Join code**. Four characters,
like `MX4T`. They sign up at **mathsense.net/student?mode=signup**, then go to
**My classes** and type it in.

> Send that link with the `?mode=signup` on the end. Without it they land on a
> login form and have to find the sign-up toggle underneath, which most people
> don't.

Two things worth knowing:

- **Inviting doesn't create accounts.** They sign up themselves — that's what
  keeps the account theirs.
- **Under 16?** Invite a parent's address and let them pass the link on.

---

## 4. Getting them answering questions

Three ways, and you don't have to pick one.

| | Sign-up needed | Kept afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Run a session** | You only | No |
| **B. Let them practise** | Both | Yes |
| **C. Set them work** | Both | Yes |

### A. Run a session — nobody signs up

The quickest way to see it working, and the easiest sell to a student who
doesn't want an account.

**Dashboard → Run a lesson → Start a session.** Pick the qualification and how
many questions each, press **Start session**. You get a code.

They go to **mathsense.net/s**, type the code and a first name. That's it. You
watch right and wrong answers come in live.

> **It stops working 6 hours after you start it**, and the whole thing is
> deleted the same evening. Great for a lesson, no use as a record.

### B. Let them practise

Once they're in a class, they can just use it: pick a skill or a topic, answer
questions, get the answer explained when they're wrong. You don't have to set
anything up — this is the main thing students do, and it's what builds their
skill map.

If you only do one thing after they've joined, tell them to go and practise.

### C. Set them specific work

**Dashboard → Assignments → Create.** Fill in **Details**, choose
**Questions**, set **Completion rules**, pick who it's **Assigned to**, then
**Create assignment**.

Worth it when you want everyone on the same thing. Not worth it for the first
week.

---

## 5. Mark a paper — no account needed at all

**mathsense.net/mark.** This is the one closest to your spreadsheet, and you can
try it right now without signing in.

You mark the paper yourself as usual. You then type the marks in, question by
question, and it tells you **which skills those dropped marks point at** — per
student, and across the group with the worst questions ranked first. Press
**Get feedback sheets** for a PDF, one page per student.

Three things:

- **Blank and zero are the same.** To leave a question out, untick **They sat
  the whole paper** and deselect it.
- **Nothing is saved.** Download the PDF before you close the tab.
- **For your first go, pick an AQA June 2025 paper.** Those sheets come with
  practice questions to hand out; most papers don't have them yet.

To have this update a student's skill map instead of being a one-off, do it from
**your class → Papers → Record a marked paper**.

---

## 6. Where to look afterwards

**Dashboard → Your students.** Two numbers each: **mastered**, and **to work
on**. Sort by **Quietest first** to find whoever has drifted.

Inside a class there's more — mastery by topic, progress over time, and
**Topics covered**, where you tick off what you've actually taught so the
percentages measure against that instead of the whole GCSE. Worth doing once
you're past the first week.

---

## Five things that will trip you up

1. **One class per student** for one-to-one.
2. **The class code goes in My classes**, on their dashboard — not on
   mathsense.net/join, which is something else.
3. **Sessions die after 6 hours.** Start a fresh one.
4. **Inviting isn't joining.** Nothing shows up until they've signed up and
   accepted.
5. **"To work on" needs five goes** at a skill before it appears, so it stays
   still at first. That's normal.
