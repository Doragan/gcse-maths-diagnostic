# Privacy notice change — user notification, September 2026

_Drafted 2026-09-22 for the v1.1 → v1.2 change. Revised the same day once it was
established that essentially no learner has joined a class. Not yet sent._

**Why this is being sent.** The published notice (§11) promises to notify
registered users of significant changes by email.

**Who it goes to: every registered account, not just those who opted into
email.** The practice-reminder opt-in is consent for marketing-style messages.
This is a service notification about a change to the terms people are already
using the product under, so withholding it from anyone who declined reminders
would defeat the purpose of the §11 promise.

**Audience.** About 80 learner accounts, almost all aged 13 to 16, and 2 teacher
accounts. Written for a fourteen-year-old: short sentences, consequence first,
no legal vocabulary. No countdown, no urgency, no loss-aversion framing, per the
same Children's Code rules that govern the rest of the learner journey.

---

## ⚠ What the recipients have actually done, and how it reorders the email

**Essentially no learner has joined a class.** That inverts the priority of the
first draft, which led on mini-exam visibility and the teacher's view of the
practice record. For virtually every recipient there is no teacher, so leading
on that would open with a paragraph about a relationship they do not have and
bury the one change that does affect them.

Reordered by what applies to the reader:

| Change | Who it reaches today |
|---|---|
| Google Analytics linked to Google Ads | Everyone who accepted the cookie banner |
| Deleting an account now cancels the subscription | The handful who pay |
| Teacher visibility, mini-exams, pre-join practice | Almost nobody, but true the moment they join |

The class paragraph stays, and stays last, framed as forward-looking. The
alternative was to leave it out and notify only the people it applies to later,
which is worse: the point of telling someone is that they know before they
decide, and the decision is whether to type in a class code.

---

## ✅ The blocker is cleared

This draft was held back because analytics consent could not be withdrawn:
`cookie-consent` was written once by the banner and read nowhere else, so anyone
who accepted had no way back. An email whose whole purpose is candour about the
advertising link cannot follow "here is what goes to Google" with "and there is
nothing you can do about it".

Fixed 2026-09-22. There is now a control on the privacy page, public and usable
without an account, which turns analytics off, stops anything further being sent
and deletes the cookies already set. Declining is also remembered now, where
before the banner returned on every visit while accept was remembered forever.

The email below says so plainly, which is the version worth sending.

## Version A — learners

**Subject:** We corrected our privacy notice

Hi,

We have updated the Mathsense privacy notice. One of the changes is a
correction, so it is worth a minute of your time.

**Google and adverts.** If you accepted analytics cookies when you first
visited, some information about how you use the site goes to Google. We
advertise Mathsense on Google, and our two accounts are linked, which means that
information can be used to measure those adverts. Our old notice said we do not
share your data with advertisers. That was not accurate, and the notice now says
what actually happens.

Your practice and your results are never part of this. It is information about
pages and clicks, not your maths. If you declined the banner, or ignored it,
nothing has ever been sent to Google.

**You can change this whenever you like.** There is a switch near the top of
mathsense.net/privacy that turns analytics on or off. Turning it off stops
anything further being sent and deletes the cookies Google has already set. You
do not need an account to use it.

**If you pay for Mathsense.** Deleting your account now cancels your
subscription at the same time, so you cannot be charged after you have gone.
That was not true before. If it happened to you, email us and we will refund it.

**If you ever join a teacher's class.** Most people using Mathsense are not in
one, so this may not apply to you yet. If you do join, your teacher can see your
whole practice record, including practice you did before joining, and can open
any mini-exam you sit and read the answers you gave. Our old notice said a
teacher could never see the answers you type. That is true of ordinary practice
and it was wrong about mini-exams, which are marked work. Joining a class is
always your choice, nobody can add you to one, and you can leave at any time
from My classes.

There is nothing you need to do. The full notice is at mathsense.net/privacy.

Chris
Mathsense — privacy@mathsense.net

## Version B — teachers

_Two recipients, neither of whom has used the product. Arguably not worth
sending at all; included so the decision is deliberate rather than an oversight._

**Subject:** Mathsense privacy notice and school data protection — what changed

Hi,

Two updates worth knowing about, both live now.

**What pupils are told about your view of them.** Our privacy notice previously
said a teacher can never see the answers a pupil types. That is true of ordinary
practice and was wrong about mini-exams, which you can open and read in full.
The notice and the class-join screen now say so. Pupils have also been told
plainly that joining a class shares their whole practice record, including
practice from before they joined. Nothing about what you can see has changed,
only what they were told about it.

**mathsense.net/dpa is no longer a data processing agreement.** It offered a
standard processor agreement describing an arrangement Mathsense does not
implement: a pupil owns their account, it exists before any school is involved,
and a school cannot create, read or delete one. The page now sets out who
controls what, and what a teacher of a class can and cannot see. If your school
needs a signed agreement, email me and we will work through it.

The full notice is at mathsense.net/privacy.

Chris
Mathsense — privacy@mathsense.net

## Before sending

- The consent control has shipped, so the analytics paragraph now points at it.
- Send from a monitored address. This invites two replies that need a human: a
  refund request, and a school asking for an agreement.
- No tracking pixel and no click measurement. A privacy-change notice is the
  worst possible place for either, and the advertising paragraph would read
  badly next to one.
- Personal signature rather than a no-reply address. It reads better to a
  teenager and matches the tone of the rest of the learner journey.
